If someone in your life talks about their dog the way other people talk about their kids, a personalised dog gift is about the safest bet in Christmas shopping. It shows you've noticed the thing they love most, and there's a good chance it'll be used every day.

This guide covers gifts for the **dog**, gifts for the **owner**, and a few things to think twice about. It's written with Australian conditions in mind: hot summers, outdoor living, and a Christmas Day that's more likely to involve a beach or backyard than a fireplace.

## Gifts for the dog (that the owner will appreciate)

### A personalised ID tag

It's not glamorous, but a clear, durable ID tag is one of the most useful things a dog can wear. If you're buying one as a gift, a few details make the difference:

- **Size it to the dog.** A big tag on a small dog is heavy and noisy; a tiny tag on a big dog is hard to read.
- **Get the text right.** Ask the owner what they'd like on it, or give a voucher instead. A wrong phone number is worse than no tag.
- **Think about noise.** Some owners prefer slide-on collar tags that don't jingle.

I've written a full guide to [what to put on a pet tag (and what to leave off)](/posts/personalised-pet-tags-what-to-engrave/), plus a [tag text checker](/tag-text-checker/) to see if your words fit.

**Note for Brisbane owners:** Brisbane City Council recommends dogs wear their council registration tag at all times, so a personalised tag is usually worn *alongside* the council tag, not instead of it.

### A personalised collar or lead

Collars and leads embroidered or printed with the dog's name and the owner's phone number are practical and look smart. Things to check:

- **Measure the neck**, don't guess. You should be able to fit two fingers under a correctly fitted collar.
- **Choose materials for the climate.** Biothane-style coated webbing and nylon dry quickly after a swim and don't get as smelly in humidity as some leathers.
- **Check the hardware.** Metal buckles and D-rings outlast plastic for strong pullers.

### A named food or water bowl

A personalised bowl is a lovely touch. Choose stainless steel or a heavy ceramic that's sold for pet use, and check whether the printed or glazed surface is dishwasher-safe. In summer, a large, stable water bowl is more useful than a pretty small one.

### A personalised bed or blanket

A cooling mat or an elevated mesh bed with the dog's name on a tag or panel suits an Aussie summer better than a thick plush bed. Check the cover is removable and washable.

## Gifts for the owner

### A pet portrait keepsake

Custom portraits come in many forms: illustrations, prints, embroidery, ceramics, phone cases and tumblers. For a good result:

- **Get a clear photo.** Front-on, eye level, good natural light, and the dog's face filling much of the frame.
- **Check colours.** Some dogs' coats are hard to capture in poor light. Daylight photos give the maker the best chance.
- **Ask for a proof** before it's made, if the seller offers one.

If you can't get a good photo without giving away the surprise, a gift voucher plus a card with the dog's photo is a perfectly good plan.

### A personalised tumbler or mug

A tumbler with the dog's portrait or name keeps water cold on long summer walks. Look for a lid that seals and a cup that fits the car cup holder.

### A walk bag or treat pouch with the dog's name

Useful for every walk: a pouch for poo bags, keys and treats, ideally with a clip for the lead. Choose wipeable fabric.

### A framed paw print or name sign

A "Biscuit's Room" or "Beware of the Very Friendly Dog" style sign is fun for the laundry door or the back gate. For outdoor signs, ask whether the materials and inks are rated for sun exposure; Queensland UV fades many prints quickly.

### A Christmas ornament with the dog's name

An ornament with the dog's name and the year is a sweet tradition, especially for a puppy's first Christmas. Hang it high if the dog likes to investigate the tree.

## What to think twice about

- **Toys with small parts or squeakers** for strong chewers. Personalised plush toys are cute, but supervise play and remove them once they start to tear.
- **Food treats from humans' Christmas tables.** Some festive foods, including chocolate, grapes, sultanas and raisins (think Christmas pudding and fruit mince) are toxic to dogs. If you're gifting treats, buy ones made for dogs, and keep the pudding well out of reach.
- **Costumes.** Some dogs tolerate them, many don't, and they can overheat a dog quickly in summer.
- **Scented candles and diffusers** as "pet-themed" gifts. Some essential oils can be harmful to pets. Check with a vet before buying for a pet household.

## Summer safety for Christmas Day

Christmas in Australia often means heat, crowds and noise. A few reminders worth passing on with your gift:

- **Hot pavement burns paws.** If you can't keep the back of your hand on the ground for several seconds, it's too hot for paws.
- **Provide shade and water** at backyard gatherings, and somewhere quiet for the dog to retreat to.
- **Watch the bin.** Ham bones, cooked bones and corn cobs are common Christmas emergencies.
- **Storms and fireworks** stress many dogs. Make sure ID tags and microchip details are up to date before New Year's Eve.

## Timing

If you're ordering personalised items, allow for making time and postage. For delivery before Christmas, check the [Australia Post Christmas sending dates](https://auspost.com.au/personal/sending/christmas-sending-dates) and order well ahead of them.

The best dog gifts are the ones that get used. A tag that helps a lost dog get home, a lead that doesn't fray, a portrait that makes the owner smile every morning: those are the ones they'll remember.
