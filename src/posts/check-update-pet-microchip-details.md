A microchip is one of the best safety nets a pet can have, but it has a weak spot most owners never think about. The chip itself doesn't hold your name, address or phone number. It holds a number. When a vet, shelter or council officer scans a found dog or cat, that number is looked up on a registry database, and the details on that database are what get your pet home.

If you've moved, changed mobile numbers, rehomed a pet, or simply can't remember who registered the chip in the first place, those details might be out of date. The run into summer is a good time to fix that. Storms, fireworks, open gates and visitors coming and going all make an escape more likely, and a chip with a disconnected phone number is close to useless.

Here's how to check it properly, in about half an hour.

## Step 1: Find the microchip number

You'll need the number before you can do anything else. Look for it in:

- the adoption, sale or transfer paperwork you received with your pet
- your vet records or vaccination certificate
- a registration certificate or email from a microchip registry
- your council registration records (some councils record it)

If you can't find it anywhere, ask your vet to scan your pet at the next visit, or ring ahead and ask whether they can do a quick scan. A scan also confirms the chip is still reading, which is worth knowing on its own.

## Step 2: Work out which registry holds it

This is where people get stuck. Australia doesn't have one single national microchip database. There are several registries, and your pet's chip could be on any of them depending on where and when it was implanted. New South Wales also runs its own state pet registry.

The easiest way to find out is [Pet Address](https://www.petaddress.com.au/), a free search site that checks a microchip number against participating Australian registries and tells you which one the chip is recorded on. It won't show you the owner details (that's private), but it points you to the right place.

Once you know the registry, go to its website or call it. Most let you log in, or create an account, and view the details held against the chip.

## Step 3: Check every field, not just the phone number

When you get into the record, read it slowly. Things that commonly go stale:

- **Primary phone number**: the big one. Make sure it's a mobile you actually answer.
- **Second contact**: a friend or family member who can be reached if you can't. Ask them first, and make sure they know your pet's name.
- **Address**: especially after a move. A found pet is often taken to a vet or shelter near where it was picked up, and the address helps them work out who to contact.
- **Owner name**: if the pet came from a breeder, rescue or previous owner, the chip may still be in their name.
- **Pet details**: name, breed, colour, sex and desexing status. Wrong details can cause confusion when a pet is scanned.
- **Email**: useful for notifications from the registry.

Fix anything that's wrong. Some registries make changes online in a few minutes; others need a form. Some charge a fee for certain changes, such as a change of owner, so check the registry's site rather than assuming it's free.

## Step 4: If the pet was transferred to you, finish the transfer

When you buy or adopt a pet, ownership on the microchip registry doesn't always change automatically. Depending on the registry, the previous owner or the rescue group may need to sign a transfer form, or you may need to submit one with proof of the sale or adoption.

If you got your pet from a rescue or shelter, they will usually handle this or give you the paperwork. If you bought from a private seller or a breeder, check that the transfer has actually been processed, not just promised. Do it now, while you can still contact the seller easily.

## Step 5: Don't forget council registration

In Queensland and most other parts of Australia, microchip registration and council registration are two separate things. Updating one doesn't update the other.

Brisbane and other councils have their own animal registration systems, with their own rules about which animals must be registered and when registrations must be renewed. If you've moved to a new council area, you'll usually need to register your pet with the new council and let the old one know. Check your council's website for the exact requirements.

## Step 6: Match the tag to the chip

A collar tag is the fast way home. A neighbour who finds your dog can ring the number on the tag straight away, without needing a scanner. The chip is the backup if the collar comes off.

So the phone number on the tag should match the number on the registry. If you've updated one, update the other. ## When to check, once it's sorted

Put a recurring reminder somewhere you'll see it. Good moments to check the chip record:

- **Every time you move house or change your mobile number.** Make it part of the same to-do list as the electricity and internet.
- **At the yearly vet check.** Ask the vet to scan the chip and confirm it reads. Chips can occasionally move from where they were implanted, and very occasionally fail.
- **Before you leave a pet with a sitter or boarding kennel.** Add the sitter as a temporary contact if your registry allows it, or at least make sure they have the chip number and registry name.
- **Before storm season and the New Year fireworks.** Noise is a classic trigger for pets bolting through gaps in fences.

## If your pet does go missing

Having the paperwork ready makes the first hours less frantic:

1. **Call the registry** to confirm your details and, if the registry offers it, mark the pet as lost.
2. **Ring your local council, nearby vets and animal shelters**, and give them the microchip number along with a description.
3. **Check pounds and shelters in person** if you can, and keep checking. Descriptions over the phone aren't always reliable.
4. **Post in local lost-pet groups** with a clear, recent photo.
5. **Search close to home first**, especially for cats, which often hide nearby.

Keep a photo of your pet's microchip number and the registry name in your phone, alongside a few clear recent photos. When you're stressed, you don't want to be hunting through a filing cabinet.

## The ten-minute version

If you only have a few minutes today:

- Find the chip number.
- Search it on Pet Address to find the registry.
- Log in and check the mobile number is current.

That one fix covers the most common problem. The rest can wait for the weekend, but don't let it wait until after a storm.
